# Discovery games and social reasons to return

Status: Product hypotheses. These are choices to explore, not a committed
feature list. Start from the small goals in
[personal radar and reminders](../goals/personal-radar.md) and the core
[positive event communities](../goals/event-communities.md).

## What is the activity for?

Three motivations could give the product a stronger identity:

1. **Explore:** find music and venues I might genuinely enjoy.
2. **Express:** build a personal record of my taste and discoveries.
3. **Contribute:** help someone else find a good show and learn from their picks.

Event conversations, shared attendance plans, positive reactions, and user
reports are core community features. These game ideas can build on the useful
interactions people already have there.

One optional social experiment is **"recommend a show to someone who will
appreciate it"**. A friend's save, positive reaction, or explanation gives the
interaction a purpose. Someone can benefit alone or with one existing friend.

## Game ideas

| Idea | Activity | Why return or involve another person? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery passport | Collect a record of distinct venues and genres explored; choose a personal next discovery. | Identity and curiosity: see your local music world grow and find the next gap you want to fill. |
| Pick swap | Send one friend one show with a sentence explaining why they might like it. The friend reacts and can send a pick back. | Thoughtfulness and reciprocity: see whether the recommendation landed, then discover something chosen for you. |
| City bingo | Complete a flexible monthly card: a new venue, an unfamiliar genre, a friend's pick, or a weekday show worth saving. | Variety and completion: a small prompt makes the next discovery easier to choose. Compare discoveries with a friend. |
| Weekly wildcard | Explore one unfamiliar artist with a relevant upcoming show; listen through an available artist link and decide whether to save it. | Surprise: a new, bounded discovery each week, followed by a real choice about a show. |
| Scout thank-you | Give credit to the person whose recommendation helped you find a worthwhile event. | Appreciation: learn that your taste helped someone, and find people whose recommendations you value. |
| Friday shortlist | A small group contributes one possible show each and responds to the resulting shortlist. | Coordination: return to see the other picks and move toward one shared plan. |
| Taste postcard | Summarize the genres, venues, and artists in your recent discoveries; share a chosen part with a friend. | Self-expression: compare interests, find an overlap, and exchange a show based on it. |
| Venue detective | Guess a venue's most-listed genre from sample event clues, then reveal the observed mix and explore related shows. | Learning and friendly comparison: understand the local scene and compare guesses. |

Use these as a menu. A passport plus one recurring activity is a sufficiently
small first experiment.

## Optional game experiment: passport + pick swap

**Personal promise:** "Keep a record of the places and music you're discovering."

**Social promise:** "Find your friend a show they'd be excited about."

An example loop:

1. Maya finds a show and adds the venue to her discovery passport.
2. She sends it to Alex: "You liked this artist's last album, and this is nearby."
3. Alex responds, "Saved it; Friday works for me."
4. Maya sees that her recommendation was useful.
5. Alex sends a pick back. They now have something new to consider or plan together.

The useful result is a recommendation that fits a person, with a response that
helps both people. The passport gives that activity a personal history. A weekly
prompt can offer another opportunity once the first exchange proves worthwhile.

### Small goals for this experiment

- A person can identify one discovery they want in their passport.
- They can name one friend who might appreciate a particular show.
- The friend understands why it was recommended and gives a meaningful response.
- At least one participant saves a relevant show or learns something useful
  about the other's taste.
- They voluntarily make a second exchange.

These outcomes are more informative than the number of badges collected or
notifications opened. Start by learning whether the exchange feels natural to a
few existing friends.

## Return moments

- **A new match:** an artist or venue the person cares about has a relevant show.
- **A timely change:** an on-sale date or saved plan needs their attention.
- **A human response:** someone replied to a question, appreciated a helpful
  contribution, or sent a pick.
- **A new prompt:** a weekly wildcard or an unfinished, self-chosen bingo goal
  offers a worthwhile next discovery.
- **A reflection:** a monthly postcard or a self-reported concert memory gives
  them something personal to keep or share.

A meaningful weekly visit is a reasonable starting hypothesis. The cadence can
follow the person's interests, the local listing supply, and responses from
friends. Returning should reveal something relevant, useful, or enjoyable.

## What makes the games feel worthwhile?

- Progress represents distinct discoveries and explicit actions. Repeating a
  save/unsave action does not create another discovery.
- Passport entries distinguish **discovered**, **saved**, and **attended**.
  Attendance and listening can be self-reported; clicks alone do not verify them.
- Discovery quests can be completed through exploration. Participation can fit
  different budgets and occasional concert-going habits.
- Prompts match available local listings and can be skipped. Progress survives
  quiet weeks, and people choose whether they want reminder prompts.
- Social recognition follows a recipient finding a recommendation useful.
- Community reactions are positive-only. Reports request private moderator
  review and do not count as negative votes or game progress.
- Taste postcards describe the user's chosen discoveries. Venue quizzes state
  the sample and period behind an answer; missing data remains explicit.

The venue detective idea could eventually connect to
[venue and price insights](venue-insights-and-discovery.md). For an optional
pick-swap experiment, the question is: **did someone help someone else find a
show they care about, and did they want to do it again?**
